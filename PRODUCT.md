# Product

## Register

product

## Users

One English-speaking owner uses the app on a personal Windows computer while writing in coding tools, browsers, documents, and chat. The primary job is to turn speech into usable text without sending audio to a hosted transcription service or interrupting the current task.

## Product Purpose

LocalDictate provides push-to-talk dictation with a small bottom-of-screen status widget. It performs speech recognition locally, inserts a faithful raw transcript immediately when requested, and can optionally pass text through the owner's signed-in Codex CLI for cleanup and rapid formatting. Success means low-friction capture, accurate English transcription, an obvious recording state, and graceful raw-text fallback whenever polishing is unavailable.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, fast, native. The app should feel like a trustworthy Windows utility that stays out of the way until invoked.

Theme packs are an explicit opt-in exception to the quiet default. A user may
choose an expressive overlay and post-processing preset without changing the
dependable behavior of transcription or the settings UI.

## Anti-references

Avoid cloud-first subscription pressure, oversized assistant surfaces, forced
attention-seeking animation, chatty status copy, and decorative AI imagery in
the default experience. Keep the useful part of Wispr Flow's compact recording
widget without imitating its branding or making the workflow dependent on a
hosted service. User-installed or explicitly selected theme packs may be
deliberately playful.

## Design Principles

1. Speech first: starting and stopping dictation must be faster than reaching for a text box.
2. Local by default: audio and recognition stay on the device; networked cleanup is explicit.
3. Never lose the words: preserve and expose the raw transcript even when polishing fails.
4. Show only live state: the overlay communicates listening, transcribing, polishing, success, or failure and then gets out of the way.
5. Familiar utility: use standard Windows interaction patterns and restrained settings rather than inventing novel controls.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The interface is keyboard-first, supports visible focus, strong text and state contrast, non-color status cues, and reduced motion. Recording and processing states must remain legible without relying on animation or sound.
